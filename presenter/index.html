Why one case in the room

One file, opened properly.

1

Focus

A guided investigation needs focus. Switching cases mid-session dilutes the “reopen the file” experience.

2

It fits the time

Envelope, evidence cards, three witnesses, Bias Button, finding: one case fits 45–60 minutes. Several cases do not.

3

Teachers see how it scales

Every case file is built the same way. Work through one and teachers can see how the other five run, from Year 4 to Year 10.

Step by step

Prompts and answer guidance

The guidance uses only the records cited in the case file and the Who Fired First? app’s Teacher Note. It is not an answer key: the record does not settle who fired first.

1

Open the envelope

5 min · condensed 4 min

Ask the room

  • Read the opener aloud. Ask: what do we think we already know about Eureka? Write it up, to test at the end.
  • Words to check: licence, stockade, sentry, signal shot, volley.

What the sources support

  • Every record agrees there was shooting before dawn on 3 December 1854. The dispute is who started it.
  • Captain Thomas led 276 men (100 mounted, 176 on foot). Inside the stockade there were far fewer diggers.
2

Read what reliable sources have established

5 min · condensed 3 min

Ask the room

  • Which source supports each fact? (The numbers lead to the bibliography.) Which detail is still disputed?

What the sources support

  • The licence burning and the Southern Cross flag on Bakery Hill (29 November) and Lalor’s oath (30 November) are established from Carboni’s account (chs XXVI–XXXII, XXXVII); the participant page’s bibliography lists every source.
  • Thomas wrote “At 3 o’clock I left”; witnesses differ on the time of the attack (Carboni woke at “full dawn, not daylight”).
  • Thirteen men, including Carboni, were tried for high treason in 1855. All were found not guilty.
3

Evidence cards

12 min · condensed 10 min

Ask the room

  • For each card: who made it, when, for whom, and were they there?
  • Which card was written closest to the event? Which was written for the public?

What the sources support

  • A. Thomas wrote the same day, to his superiors. He says his men were fired on “without word or challenge”, and that only then did he order “commence firing”. He does not mention a warning shot from a sentry.
  • B. Lalor wrote four months later, to the newspapers: “I believe that one or two signal shots were fired by our sentries”, and “The military fired the first volley.” He separates a signal shot from a volley, and hedges with “I believe”.
  • C. Carboni, a year later: he woke to “a discharge of musketry—then a round from the bugle”. He does not say which side fired.
  • D. The Argus of 4 December was the first report in Melbourne. No complete set of the Ballarat Times from 1854 survives.
  • E. The State Library Victoria diary was once wrongly credited to Samuel Lazarus; it is now attributed to Charles Evans. It records a death: preview it before sharing.
4

Three witnesses

12 min · condensed 10 min

Ask the room

  • What did they say they saw? Could they really have seen it? Why might they say it?
  • Where was each witness standing, and what could they not see in the half-light?

What the sources support

  • Lalor was inside the stockade, as the diggers’ leader. Thomas commanded the attacking force outside it. Carboni was asleep in a tent “the second from the stockade”.
  • Strong answers notice that Thomas wrote for his superiors, that Lalor chooses careful words, and that Carboni heard the firing but does not name a side.
  • When quoting, check the originals: some modern summaries change Lalor’s “fifteen yards” to “fifteen feet” and his “twenty with pikes” to thirty.
5

Bias Button

8 min · condensed 7 min

Ask the room

  • Who made it, and why? Who is missing? What are the loaded words doing? What does it want you to think or do?
  • Ask for one loaded word and one missing voice from the room.

What the sources support

  • Thomas calls the diggers “insurgents”. Lalor writes “our sentries”. Each word places the writer on a side.
  • Missing voices: the sentries are never named; the women of the goldfields appear only in later notices. No source in this case says Anastasia Hayes saw the first shot.
6

Decide: our finding

8 min · condensed 7 min

Ask the room

  • Rebels, soldiers, or we can’t know: which piece of evidence most changed your mind? How sure are you?

What the sources support

  • There is no answer key. Historians still disagree about who fired first.
  • Strong findings use more than one witness, separate a signal shot from a volley, say who could see what, and say how sure they are. “We can’t know” is a legitimate finding.
  • Nobody knows exactly how many died: diggers, soldiers, and at least one woman.
7

Close the file

5 min · condensed 4 min

Ask the room

  • Return to the list from the start. What changed? What would we need to settle the question?
  • How did Eureka affect the way the colony was governed?

What the sources support

  • The stockade was taken in minutes. Lalor was badly wounded and hidden by friends. The thirteen accused were found not guilty. The licence was soon replaced, and Lalor was later elected to Victoria’s parliament.

Take it to the classroom

A ready-to-use approach

Presenter note. The same structure works across Years 4 to 10. Eureka runs well with the whole room; in class, teachers choose the case for their year level, from Deep Time and Batavia (Year 4) to the Freedom Ride (Year 10). Frontier (the Bathurst War and Myall Creek) is suited to Year 8/9.
5 min

Open the envelope

Read the opener aloud. Ask: what do we think we already know? Write it up, to test later.

10 min

Read what is established

Students mark what reliable sources have established and what is still disputed. Teach any new words.

15 min

Evidence stations

Print the evidence cards. Groups rotate, noting who made each source, when, and for whom.

15 min

Ask three witnesses

Same three questions for every witness. Compare answers across groups.

10 min

Bias Button

Each group presses the button on one source and reports one loaded word and one missing voice.

10 min

Decide

Each student writes a finding, one piece of evidence, one thing we can’t know, and sure, maybe or guess.

5 min

Close the file

Return to the opening list. What changed? What would we need to settle the question?

Suggested classroom timings: about 70 minutes in all. For two lessons, stop after the witnesses.

Investigation roles

Lead Investigator

Keeps the case question in front of the team, and keeps time.

Archivist

Tracks every source: who made it, when, where it is held, and the link.

Witness Examiner

Asks each witness the three questions, and asks “how do you know?”

Bias Checker

Runs the Bias Button and flags loaded words and missing voices.

Recorder

Writes the team’s finding and the “We can’t know” list.

Teacher Note

Year levels. The same structure works across Years 4 to 10. Year levels come from each app’s own Teacher Note: Deep Time and Batavia, Year 4; Eureka, Year 5; Federation, Year 6; Freedom Ride, Year 10. Frontier is suited to Year 8/9; the app’s own note maps it to Australian Curriculum v9 Year 9 History (AC9HH9K03), so check the fit for your class. Participant pages carry no year level.

What is true on these pages. Every date, place and quotation comes from the cited records, as already checked for the live Reopen the Record apps, or from the linked public source. Nothing is invented. Several apps open with a page labelled Imagined (for Eureka, Nell’s Diary). Those openers are not reproduced on the workshop pages. Tell students which parts of an app are imagined, and ask how they could check.

Cultural safety. Follow the AIATSIS Code of Ethics and the protocols of the local communities whose Countries and histories each case concerns. Eureka took place on Wadawurrung Country. There is no secret or sacred material, ceremony, retold cultural story, rock art image or photograph of a deceased Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person on these pages; silhouettes are used.

Violence and harmful language. Men died at Eureka, and at least one woman’s death is recorded. Some linked reports, memoirs and diaries describe wounds and deaths in detail, including the Charles Evans diary: preview them before class. Racist historical material in the other cases is linked or quoted as a short, attributed extract with a content note.

An apolitical approach. The routine asks what a source can prove, not what to think. Frame modern comparisons in the Bias Button as a study of published language, not a verdict on a person.

Corrections. If something here should be corrected, it will be updated. The note asking for corrections is permanent.

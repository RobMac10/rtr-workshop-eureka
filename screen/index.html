Nell’s Diary

Gunfire at Dawn

Exhibit N: diary of Nell Byrne, Ballarat, 1854

Imagined Written for this case, based on real events, not a real document.

Open the envelope

Before dawn on Sunday 3 December 1854, soldiers and police marched from the Government Camp at Ballarat to a stockade built by gold diggers on the Eureka lead.

Captain Thomas led 276 men. Inside the stockade there were far fewer diggers.

Shooting broke out. Within minutes it was over.

Everyone agrees there was shooting. Not everyone agrees who started it.

Envelope contents · 3

What reliable sources have established

  • During 1854 police checked digger licences, and the diggers grew angry.
  • On 29 November a huge meeting on Bakery Hill burned licences and raised the blue Southern Cross flag.
  • On 30 November Peter Lalor led the diggers in an oath under the flag.
  • Thomas’s force of 276 men (100 mounted, 176 on foot) left the Government Camp early on 3 December. The exact time is disputed.
  • Thirteen men, including Raffaello Carboni, were tried for high treason in 1855. All were found not guilty.

Evidence A

Official report · same day

Captain J. W. Thomas’s report to Head Quarters

“were received by rather sharp and well-directed fire from the insurgents, without word or challenge on their part; then, and not till then, I ordered the bugle to sound the ‘commence firing;’”

Look closer. Written the same day, for his superiors. What did he need them to believe?

Captain J. W. Thomas, report to Head Quarters, Camp, Ballaarat, 3 December 1854. Government records held by Public Record Office Victoria; transcription on Wikisource.

Evidence B

Letter to the newspapers · 1855

Peter Lalor, “To the Colonists of Victoria”

“I believe that one or two signal shots were fired by our sentries.”

Look closer. He also wrote: “The military fired the first volley.” Notice “I believe”. What is he sure of, and what is he not?

The Argus, 10 April 1855, p. 7 (also the Geelong Advertiser, 9 April 1855).

Evidence C

Eyewitness book · 1855

Raffaello Carboni, The Eureka Stockade

“I awoke. Sunday morning. It was full dawn, not daylight. A discharge of musketry—then a round from the bugle—the command ‘forward’.”

Look closer. He heard firing first, then the bugle. Does he say which side fired?

Raffaello Carboni, The Eureka Stockade (Melbourne, 1855), chapter LVI. Free full text at Project Gutenberg, eBook 3546.

Evidence D

First Melbourne report · 4 December 1854

“By Express. Fatal Collision at Ballaarat”

The first report to reach Melbourne readers, printed the day after the battle.

Look closer. Who could a Melbourne reporter talk to within a day? Who could he not?

The Argus, 4 December 1854, p. 5. No complete set of the Ballarat Times from 1854 survives.

Evidence E

Diary · 1853–1855

Charles Evans’s goldfields diary

A diary kept on the goldfields at the time, held by State Library Victoria.

Look closer. A diary was once credited to the wrong person. What does that teach us about checking who made a source?

State Library Victoria, “An eyewitness account of the Eureka Stockade”. Once wrongly said to be by Samuel Lazarus. It records a death.

Three witnesses, three questions

Ask every witness the same three questions.

Peter Lalor

Captain John Wellesley Thomas

Raffaello Carboni

  1. What did they say they saw?
  2. Could they really have seen it?
  3. Why might they say it?

Witness 1

Peter Lalor

The diggers’ leader · inside the stockade

“I believe that one or two signal shots were fired by our sentries.” He also wrote: “The military fired the first volley.” A signal shot is a warning; a volley is many guns fired together.

Source. Peter Lalor, “To the Colonists of Victoria”, The Argus, 10 April 1855, p. 7.

Witness 2

Captain John Wellesley Thomas

Commander of the soldiers and police

His men “were received by rather sharp and well-directed fire from the insurgents, without word or challenge on their part”. His report does not mention a warning shot from a sentry.

Source. Captain J. W. Thomas, report to Head Quarters, 3 December 1854.

Witness 3

Raffaello Carboni

An Italian digger · asleep in a tent beside the stockade

His tent was “the second from the stockade, on the hill, west.” “A discharge of musketry—then a round from the bugle—the command ‘forward’.” He does not say which side fired that first discharge.

Source. Raffaello Carboni, The Eureka Stockade (1855), chapter LVI.

  4. What does it want you to think or do?
